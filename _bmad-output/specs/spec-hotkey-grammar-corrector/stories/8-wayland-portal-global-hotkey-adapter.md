## Intent

**Problem:** `WaylandPortalGlobalHotkey` is an honest stub: every `bind()` resolves to `HotkeyUnavailable` and `activations` never emits. Story 7 put a real grab behind the X11 adapter; on a Wayland session — the display server most new Linux desktops now default to — CAP-1's summon and CAP-12's Wayland half still reach nothing, and AD-9's second adapter is the last one in the epic with no backend.

**Approach:** Implement AD-11's four-step `org.freedesktop.portal.GlobalShortcuts` handshake over `package:dbus`, inside the single file the spine's Structural Seed names, and drive it in tests over a **real D-Bus wire**: `package:dbus` ships its own in-process `DBusServer`, so a fake portal *service* can host the whole sequence under plain `dart test` with no `dbus-daemon`, no compositor and no skip. A pure-Dart `XdgShortcutTrigger` serializes the domain binding to the shortcuts-spec trigger syntax, and every way the portal can refuse — including a bind the compositor silently discards — resolves to a value.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:**
- **AD-11, the order, at the wire.** Exactly `Registry.Register` on `/org/freedesktop/host/portal/registry` → `GlobalShortcuts.CreateSession` on `/org/freedesktop/portal/desktop` → `BindShortcuts(session, [("toggle-panel", {description, preferred_trigger})], parent_window: "", options)` → subscribe `Activated` and `ShortcutsChanged`. `Register` is issued **once per process** and never re-issued on a rebind. `CreateSession` passes both `handle_token` and `session_handle_token`; each `Response` is awaited on the Request path the call returned.
- **AD-11's Registry tolerance.** `DBusUnknownMethodException` and `DBusServiceUnknownException` from `Register` are non-events — portal < 1.20 has no Registry. Any *other* Register failure is one error-level log and the sequence continues: Register is advisory app-id association, and AD-12 names only `CreateSession` and `BindShortcuts` as the steps whose failure means no hotkey.
- **AD-10.** `bind()` answers `HotkeyBound(HotkeyRegistration(effective: null, authority: BindingAuthority.compositor))` on success. `effective` is null because the portal reports the bound shortcut only as a localized `trigger_description`, never as a machine-readable trigger — the exact case AD-10's "null when the backend cannot report it" exists for. Never synthesize `effective` from the request.
- **AD-11 / AD-12, the read-back.** `toggle-panel` must appear in the `shortcuts` the Response carries back. The portal documents that list as "a subset of the shortcuts which were passed in … (this includes the set of all shortcuts and the empty set)", so an id asked for and not returned is **not bound** → `HotkeyUnavailable`, and its message names the reverse-DNS app id and its `.desktop` entry, which is the documented reason GNOME discards a bind.
- **AD-12.** `bind()` never throws and never rejects. An absent session bus, an absent portal, an absent GlobalShortcuts backend, a non-zero `Response`, a missing `session_handle`, a reply-signature mismatch and a disposed adapter are all values.
- **AD-9.** `package:dbus` is named by **exactly one** file under `lib/` — `wayland_portal_global_hotkey.dart`. No `DBusValue`, object path, interface name, error name or trigger string appears above `lib/src/infrastructure/hotkey/`.
- **AD-1, in its sharp local form:** `wayland_portal_global_hotkey.dart` must not acquire a Flutter import, directly or transitively. `test/infrastructure/system/daemon_startup_test.dart` imports it and runs under `dart test`, which cannot resolve `dart:ui`.
- **AD-4.** `dispose()` closes the portal session, cancels both signal subscriptions, closes the `DBusClient` and closes `activations`; it is idempotent and never throws. Closing the client is not tidiness — `package:dbus` documents that an unclosed client can stop the Dart process terminating, which on the Wayland branch would be a daemon that never exits.
- **AD-8 / CAP-1.** An `Activated` reaches `activations` with nothing allocated, loaded or awaited on the path. `PanelController` is untouched.
- **The sandbox rule, as a marked seam and nothing more.** Ship the non-sandboxed path: the Register step is one named, documented method stating that a sandboxed (Flatpak) app must not call it and naming story 11 plus the ledger entry. Ship **no sandbox detector** — `/.dockerenv` exists in this container (story 6 established that `tray_manager`'s own detector fires here), so a detector would take the sandboxed branch in every test and make the Register path dead code.
- Consistency Conventions: adapters named for their technology, one public type per file, the house `_log` swallow on every recovery-path log call, a caught error reduced to `error_type` and never its `toString()`, and never a log line carrying user text.
- AGENTS.md §8: nothing is faked. A claim this container cannot observe is stated as unobserved and its test is unconditionally skipped with a `fail()` body.
- Every test outside `test/platform/` runs with no Flutter binding and cites its CAP or AD id.

**Block If:**
- Honouring AD-10 or AD-11 turns out to require editing `HotkeyRegistration`, `BindingAuthority`, `HotkeyBinding`, `HotkeyBindOutcome` or the `GlobalHotkey` port. Those are spine-verbatim (AD-9) or settled by story 3. Two known pressures are **file, do not build**: surfacing the portal's `trigger_description`, and pushing a `ShortcutsChanged` rebind upward, both need a port surface that does not exist and that story 10 is the consumer of.
- The in-process `DBusServer` turns out unable to host the fake portal reliably — per-test bus setup and teardown, `requestName`, cross-client signal routing. It was probed green here (see Design Notes), so this is a guard rather than an expectation; if it fails, the fallback is story 7's `HotkeyRegistrar`-shaped seam, and trading a real-wire gate for a fake-seam gate is not an unattended choice.
- Keeping `wayland_portal_global_hotkey.dart` Flutter-free turns out to require moving AD-9's display-server → adapter selection out of `DaemonStartup` and into `main.dart`'s body, where no test can reach it.

**Never:**
- No X11 work. `x11_global_hotkey.dart`, `hotkey_registrar.dart`, `hotkey_manager_registrar.dart`, `hotkey_grab.dart` and `hotkey_key_catalogue.dart` are read-only here.
- No settings screen (story 10), no panel widgets (story 9), no `.desktop` file and no packaging (story 11).
- No new dependency and no pubspec pin change — `dbus 0.7.14` is already pinned and already in the lock file.
- No edits under `lib/src/domain/**` or `lib/src/application/**`.
- **No parsing of `trigger_description`** into a `HotkeyBinding`. It is user-readable, locale-dependent and backend-specific; a parse would be a prediction of vendor behaviour that misreports silently (AGENTS.md §1).
- No timeout on a portal `Response`. AD-11 makes a portal dialog the user must answer normal, and cancelling it out from under them would be worse; a user who dismisses it produces a non-zero `Response`, which *is* handled. The narrow case — a portal that answers never — is filed, not guessed at.
- No subscription to the Session `Closed` signal, no automatic re-bind, no retry, and no fallback to the X11 adapter (AGENTS.md §8 rules out cascades).
- No `ListShortcuts` call. AD-11's read-back is the `BindShortcuts` Response.
- No test that could pass in this container for anything needing a real compositor, a real portal backend or a real key press.
- Do not fix the filed ledger entries riding on these files: DW-17 (the startup bind outcome never reaches the settings surface), the tray never being told about a rebind, `SettingsController`'s bind-before-write divergence and its mutation-generation race. They stay filed — note in the completion notes that this story is what makes the last two live on Wayland as well as X11, and that the mutation-generation race is *worse* here, because a portal dialog makes the window seconds wide by design.

Where the two differ (a refused key, `effective`) the differences are deliberate and are in Design Notes. **Read-only.**
- `lib/src/infrastructure/hotkey/hotkey_key_catalogue.dart` -- the X11 half of AD-9's serialization, and the source of truth for **which key labels this app offers** (63: 26 letters, 10 digits, F1–F12, 15 named). B9 pins the new table against `labels`. **Read-only.**
- `lib/src/infrastructure/system/daemon_startup.dart` -- `_hotkeyFor` at line ~237 constructs the adapter; the Wayland arm gains `logger:`. `bindHotkey`/`requestBinding` already reduce a throwing adapter to a value and already tell the tray. Otherwise unchanged.
- `lib/main.dart` -- **unchanged.** The adapter owns its own `DBusClient`, so no new vendor object reaches the composition root, and both teardown paths (`DaemonLifecycle.shutdown()` step and `_releaseWithoutLifecycle`) already dispose `startup.hotkey`, which is what closes the client.
- **`dbus 0.7.14`, the six facts this design rests on** — each measured here, not assumed:
  1. `DBusClient` is inert until its first call: `callMethod` awaits a lazy `_connect()` (`lib/src/dbus_client.dart:614`), so constructing one on an X11 session costs nothing — the same property story 7 relied on for `HotkeyManagerRegistrar`.
  2. `package:dbus` ships an **in-process message bus**: `DBusServer.listenAddress(DBusAddress.unix(dir: …))` (`lib/src/dbus_server.dart:379`). Probed: two clients on one such bus do `requestName`, `registerObject`, method calls with error replies, and cross-client signal delivery; five buses were stood up and torn down in one process without tripping DW-15's unix-rebind hazard, which is specific to the abstract namespace.
  3. The error types are exactly the ones AD-11 and AD-12 name. Measured against both the in-process bus and a real `dbus-daemon`: a destination nobody owns → `DBusServiceUnknownException`; an unimplemented member on a live object → `DBusUnknownMethodException`; a bad path → `DBusUnknownObjectException`; no bus socket at all → `SocketException`.
  4. **Subscribe-then-call is ordering-safe on one client.** `DBusSignalStream._onListen` registers the stream in the client synchronously and fires `AddMatch` before the caller's next `callMethod` reaches the socket, so the bus installs the rule before it forwards the call. Probed with a fake portal that emits `Response` in the same turn it answers the call, and no delay: the signal is never missed. This is what makes A15 a property rather than a hope.
  5. `DBusClient.session()` reads `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS`, falling back to `/run/user/<uid>/bus`. Neither exists here, so the shipped default fails with `SocketException` — matrix row A7 is the state of this container.
  6. The package warns that an unclosed client can stop the Dart process terminating. `dispose()` closing it is load-bearing, not hygiene (the same shape as the already-filed `AppDatabase` lifecycle entry).
- **The portal contract, from the published specs** — `CreateSession(a{sv})→o`, `BindShortcuts(o, a(sa{sv}), s, a{sv})→o`, `Activated(o, s, t, a{sv})`, `ShortcutsChanged(o, a(sa{sv}))`. Request shortcut keys are `description` and `preferred_trigger`; the returned ones are `description` and `trigger_description` — **user-readable text, not a trigger**. Two sentences decide the design: "An application can only attempt bind shortcuts of a session once", and the `shortcuts` result is "a subset of the shortcuts which were passed in … (this includes … the empty set)".
- **The trigger syntax**, from the freedesktop shortcuts specification: modifiers `CTRL, ALT, SHIFT, NUM, LOGO` "as defined as `XKB_MOD_NAME_*`", joined by `+`, with the key an identifier "taken from `xkbcommon-keysyms.h` without the `XKB_KEY_*` prefix"; examples `CTRL+a`, `CTRL+SHIFT+a`, `CTRL+ALT+Return`. Every keysym name in row B6 was verified by calling `xkb_keysym_from_name` against the installed xkbcommon 1.6.0; `Space`, `Enter`, `Backspace` and `ArrowUp` return `NoSymbol`, and `Page_Up` resolves but canonicalizes to `Prior`.
- `test/infrastructure/hotkey/x11_global_hotkey_test.dart` -- the row-per-matrix-line idiom to copy, including the CAP/AD id in every name.
- `test/infrastructure/hotkey/stub_hotkey_adapters_test.dart` -- its own doc promises that "the day either grows a real backend, its half of this suite is what says the degradation was replaced rather than merely renamed". Story 7 took the X11 half; this story takes the last one, so the file goes.
- `test/architecture/hotkey_confinement_test.dart` -- the AD-1 scan to extend, including its "the scan actually finds something" row.
- `test/platform/x11_hotkey_live_test.dart`, `test/platform/tray_live_test.dart` -- the unconditionally-skipped-with-`fail()`-body idiom for row C.
- `test/support/value_equality.dart`, `test/fakes/throwing_logger.dart`, `test/fakes/fake_logger.dart` -- the shared helpers rows A24 and B need.
- `dart_test.yaml` -- `concurrency: 1` is already set (DW-15), which is also what keeps many in-process buses from overlapping across suites.
- `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/deferred-work.md` -- append-only. This story closes nothing and files the findings named in Tasks.

## Design Notes

**Why this story does not use the seam idiom stories 5, 6 and 7 established.** `PanelWindow`, `TrayIcon` and `HotkeyRegistrar` exist for one reason: their vendor packages are Flutter plugins behind method channels, so merely naming them needs a binding and the decision logic above them would otherwise be unreachable from `dart test`. `package:dbus` is pure `dart:io` — and it ships its own in-process message bus. So the test need that justifies a one-implementation abstraction (AGENTS.md §4.2) is **absent here**: every claim the dispatch asks for — the exact call order, the Registry tolerance of `UnknownMethod`/`ServiceUnknown`, the read-back of effective bindings, the `HotkeyUnavailable` degradation — is observable over a real D-Bus wire against a fake portal *service*, which is a strictly stronger fake than a fake client and asserts at the boundary that actually exists. A seam would put our own abstraction between the assertion and the wire and pin less. It also means `package:dbus` lands in exactly the file the spine's Structural Seed names for it, which story 7 could not manage for `hotkey_manager`.

**Why `effective` is null, and why that is a measurement rather than a shortcut.** AD-10 says `bind()` returns the effective binding read back from the portal. What the portal actually returns per shortcut is `description` and `trigger_description`, the latter documented as "user-readable text describing how to trigger the shortcut for the client to render" — localized, backend-specific, and not a trigger. There is no machine-readable combination in the reply. Reconstructing a `HotkeyBinding` from that string would be a prediction of vendor and locale behaviour that fails silently, which is exactly what AGENTS.md §1 rules out and what story 7 declined to do for the keybinder accelerator. AD-10 already has the vocabulary for this case — "null when the backend cannot report it" — so the adapter reports null, logs the description, and the gap between AD-10's *display* requirement and `HotkeyRegistration`'s shape is filed for story 10, which is the surface that needs it.

**The read-back is the story's load-bearing check, not a formality.** The portal documents the returned `shortcuts` as a subset of what was passed in, explicitly including the empty set. So "we asked for `toggle-panel` and it is not in the reply" is a first-class, documented outcome — and it is precisely how GNOME reports a bind it discarded because the app id has no matching installed `.desktop` file. Turning that into `HotkeyUnavailable`, with a message naming the app id and the desktop entry, is what converts AD-11's most-likely silent failure into AD-12's visible degradation. Until story 11 ships `com.divertedriver.HotkeyGrammarCorrector.desktop`, that is the path a real GNOME session is expected to take — which is a correct report, not a bug in this story.

**Why a rebind is a whole new session.** "An application can only attempt bind shortcuts of a session once." So CAP-12's rebind cannot re-issue `BindShortcuts`; it must close the session and build another — the Wayland analogue of X11's release-then-grab, and it fails the same way. A refused `Session.Close` therefore **abandons** the rebind rather than creating a second live session: two sessions would leave the compositor holding two shortcuts with only one of them filtered in, which is the double-binding shape story 7 spent a review pass on. `Register` is the exception and is latched per process, because AD-11 says once.

**Why the Request subscription precedes the call it awaits.** The portal's own convention exists because of a documented race: a caller that subscribes to `Response` only after the method reply can miss a signal already emitted. Subscribing first closes it, and fact 4 above shows the ordering holds on one client with no delay. This does not bend AD-11's order — AD-11's four steps are the *portal operations*, and awaiting a `Response` is part of steps 2 and 3; step 4 is `Activated`/`ShortcutsChanged`, and row A18 is what pins that those two are still subscribed last.

**Why an unrepresentable key omits the trigger instead of refusing it (A26), where X11 refuses (its A4).** On X11 the app owns the grab, so a key it cannot express is a shortcut that cannot exist and reporting success would be a lie. Under the portal the app owns nothing: `preferred_trigger` is optional and advisory, the compositor and user choose the combination, and the portal shows its own dialog. Refusing would deny the user a working shortcut over a preference we merely could not phrase. The two adapters answer differently because the authority differs, which is the whole point of AD-10.

**`meta` maps to `LOGO`, and here that is unambiguous.** The shortcuts spec's modifier set is `CTRL, ALT, SHIFT, NUM, LOGO` as defined by `XKB_MOD_NAME_*`; there is no `META` or `SUPER`, and the installed header gives `XKB_MOD_NAME_LOGO = "Mod4"` — the modifier a Linux Super key actually produces. Worth stating because it is the *opposite* of the X11 side, where story 7 filed `HotkeyModifier.meta` reaching keybinder as GDK's virtual `GDK_META_MASK` rather than Mod4 as an open, unmeasurable question. On this adapter that axis is settled by the spec.

**Why no sandbox detector.** The spine's Deferred section says a sandboxed app must not call `Registry.Register`, and packaging format is undecided. A detector would have to key off the same signals `tray_manager`'s does — and story 6 established that one of them, `/.dockerenv`, is true in this container. Shipping it would take the sandboxed branch in every test run and leave the Register step, the first invariant in AD-11, executed by nothing. So the non-sandboxed path ships, the rule is written on the one method that would change, and the decision is filed for whoever picks a package format.

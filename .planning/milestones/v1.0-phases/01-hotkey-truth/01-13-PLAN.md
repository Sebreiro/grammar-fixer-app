## Two new routes, for UAT test 14's `retest_after_fix` — and what they can and cannot prove

Its concern: presses 1 and 3 ended on `FocusIn(NotifyUngrab)` and 2 and 4 on `FocusIn(NotifyNormal)`,
and a GDK that dropped the `NotifyUngrab` form would leave `_focused` false, so the next genuine blur
would be discarded by DW-33 — the withdrawn G-01-14 symptom, for real. Plan 01-12 removes that
dependency by leaving `_focused` true on the suppressed path.

**Read what follows honestly, because the register matters more than the result.** These two routes
are **regression guards, not fail-first evidence**, and three separate things are true of them:

- They have **no pre-fix baseline**, because plan 01-11 did not carry them.
- Per UAT test 14's own HEAD evidence, both would have read HIDE *before* the fix as well — the blur
  path was healthy at HEAD, and every one of its four routes dismissed. So a post-fix HIDE
  **discriminates nothing**: it is the same answer the broken build gave.
- The mechanism they target is **hypothetical and has never reproduced in this container**. Nobody
  has observed GDK dropping the `NotifyUngrab` form here; it was inferred from presses 1 and 3
  differing from 2 and 4.

So what they buy is the absence of a symptom after a change that could plausibly have introduced
one, and that is worth having — plan 01-12 moves the `_focused` clear, which is exactly the kind of
edit that breaks a dismissal three gestures later. They do not exercise the mechanism, and neither
this plan's `must_haves` nor the observation record may claim they do.

**`steal-after-presses`.** Exercise the suppressed focus-out several times, then have a real second
toplevel take the keyboard, and require the panel to go away.

*The trap this route sets for itself.* Post-fix each press **toggles**, so whether the panel is
mapped when the steal lands depends on press-count parity and on the starting state. Pre-fix it
never hid, so it was mapped whatever the count. A route that fires four presses and then steals
therefore reads HIDE on the broken build and NOTHING on the fixed one — and NOTHING would trip this
plan's own "do not adjust the expectation, hand it back" rule over a planning error rather than a
defect.

*So the mapped panel is an invariant of the route, not a side effect of the press count.* The route
must: fire its presses; then **assert the panel is mapped, event-confirmed**, issuing one further
press and waiting for its `MapNotify` if it is not; only then steal. Classify from
`WINDOW=post-steal` — events whose timestamp follows the steal — and print `PRESTEAL=mapped`
immediately before `WINDOW=` on the result line, so the invariant is visible in the output rather
than assumed by a reader. That position is not cosmetic: this route's gate matches
`PRESTEAL=mapped` and `WINDOW=post-steal` as an adjacent ordered pair, per the single-space field
contract plan 01-11 fixed.

**`press-then-click`.** UAT test 14's fourth route: a press, then a desktop click, three attempts.

*The trap here is worse, because the naive form passes for the wrong reason.* Post-fix the press at
a mapped panel dismisses the panel itself. The click then lands on an already-unmapped window and
contributes nothing — but the whole-press window still shows `UNMAP=1 MAP=0`, so a `VERDICT=HIDE`
gate goes green while the unmap it counted belongs to the toggle. The route would assert the click
still dismisses while never testing the click at all.

*So the route re-establishes a mapped panel between the press and the click, and classifies only
from what follows the click.* Sequence, each step event-confirmed before the next: press at a mapped
panel → confirm the panel went away → press again → confirm `MapNotify` → **click** → classify from
`WINDOW=post-click`. The unmap counted is then unambiguously the click's.

*And note what that costs, because it is a finding rather than a workaround.* The state UAT test 14
worried about — a suppressed focus-out with the panel still mapped — is **no longer reachable by
that gesture at all**, because the press now hides the panel. The one route that still produces it
is `foreign-grab`, which delivers a suppressed focus-out with no activation and leaves the panel up.
If that mechanism ever does reproduce, composing `foreign-grab` with a click is the probe that would
exercise it. Say this in the observation record; do not add the route here.

**The AD-18 consequence, and how to see it.** Before the fix the departure recorded for a press at a
visible panel was `focusLost`, and `correction_controller.dart:401` leaves `_dismissalStands`
untouched for `focusLost`, so the following `shown` began no fresh session and the editor kept its
text. After the fix the press produces a real `hide()` request, whose departure is `dismissed`, so
the next `shown` clears the panel and re-seeds from the clipboard. Recipe, using `xclip`, which is
installed:

```
DISPLAY=:99 printf 'ZZALPHA111' | xclip -selection clipboard
# press to show; the editor should read ZZALPHA111
DISPLAY=:99 printf 'QQBETA222' | xclip -selection clipboard
# press to hide, press to show; the editor should now read QQBETA222
```
Before the fix the second summon kept `ZZALPHA111`. Capture both states with `import`/`scrot` on the
toplevel. This is a human-check, not an automated gate: reading text off a screenshot is not
something to gate a run on, and `workflow.human_verify_mode` is `end-of-phase`.

**The observation record.** `test/platform/panel-toggle-observation.md`, in the register the two
existing files in that directory use. It is a record of an observation **made**, not a procedure for
one that is **owed**, so it is deliberately not added to `runtime_checklists_test.dart`'s
`_procedures` list — that list pins the manual procedures the container cannot run, and this is the
opposite kind of document. Say so in the file, or a later reader will file it in the wrong place.

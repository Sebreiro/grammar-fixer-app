## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Content Generation — structured grammar correction

**Description:**
The resident Flutter daemon sends text from its editable panel to exactly one configured correction provider and streams formal, casual, and shorter variants. This phase adds an OpenAI-compatible provider for a user-configured OpenAI API or local Ollama endpoint while keeping the shipped Claude Agent SDK sidecar as the default. A good result improves English fluency, preserves the writer's meaning, and reaches the panel in the existing structured suggestion shape.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A correction changes the writer's intended facts, obligations, or tone without making that change apparent.
2. The adapter sends text or a secret to an endpoint the user did not configure, logs sensitive content, or creates an API key in config from Settings, keyring, or environment. D-17 preserves a key the user hand-placed in config on later saves.
3. A malformed or incomplete stream is presented as a completed register variant or persisted in a different suggestion shape.
4. A hidden panel or second correction leaves a request running or publishes late events from the abandoned session.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Personal writing assistance / English language support.

**User Population:** People who write in English as an additional language and want a quick correction while composing a message in another desktop app. They can edit the draft, inspect the original beside the suggestions, choose a register, and explicitly copy a suggestion.

**Stakes Level:** Medium. The user reviews and chooses the text, but a copied suggestion may become an external message with changed meaning or unintended disclosure.

**Output Consequence:** One of three suggestions (formal, casual, shorter) may be pasted and sent as the user's own words; the same structured suggestions and source text are retained in local correction history.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Intended meaning and commitments**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every variant keeps the writer's actors, negation, uncertainty, dates, quantities, names, and requested action. “I may attend Friday” remains tentative and Friday remains the date.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A correction turns “may” into “will,” drops “not,” changes who must act, or invents a deadline or promise.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** [Asano et al., grammaticality, fluency, and meaning preservation in correction evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/I17-2058/); product [CAP-3 register variants of the same correction](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md).

**Dimension: Correct English without unnecessary rewriting**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Fix actual agreement, tense, article, preposition, spelling, or punctuation errors and produce idiomatic English, while leaving already acceptable wording and the writer's voice intact.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Leave a clear learner error in place, introduce a new error, or replace an acceptable sentence with a different idea merely to sound more polished.  
**Stakes:** Medium  
**Source:** [Asano et al., combined grammaticality and fluency criteria](https://aclanthology.org/I17-2058/); [Zeng et al., overcorrection in advanced learner writing](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.569/).

**Dimension: Register fit**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** The formal option is suitable for a professional recipient; the casual option sounds natural in an informal exchange; both make the same request or statement and remain grammatical.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The formal option adds an apology or commitment the writer did not make, the casual option is rude or stiff, or the two options are effectively the same wording under different labels.  
**Stakes:** Medium  
**Source:** Product [CAP-3 and CAP-4](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md) require labeled, side-by-side register variants; register appropriateness is a writing-practice judgment for human reviewers.

**Dimension: Brevity without omission**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** The shorter option removes repetition while retaining every material request, condition, exception, deadline, and recipient-relevant fact.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** It drops “unless approved,” a quantity, or a requested reply, or becomes a fragment that cannot be sent on its own.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** Product [CAP-3 shorter variant](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md); [Agrawal and Carpuat, human checks for meaning preservation in shortened text](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.24/) (applied here by inference to the shorter correction).

**Dimension: Draft confidentiality**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Only the current draft goes to the one selected provider; no draft, suggestion body, clipboard text, or key appears in logs, and correction history remains local.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** An earlier draft is carried into a later correction, content or a key appears in diagnostics, or history is sent to another service.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** Phase [privacy constraint and stateless provider acceptance](02-SPEC.md), product [local history and single-provider rules](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md), and [GDPR Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679) where personal data processing falls within its scope.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Overcorrection of fluent learner prose:** the model makes stylistic substitutions where no correction is needed, flattening the writer's voice. This is documented particularly for advanced learners by [Zeng et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.569/) and as a broader LLM precision problem by [Park et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1431/).
- **Surface correction with a semantic mistake:** tense, preposition, or idiom is mishandled, or a polished rewrite changes negation, certainty, the actor, or a date. Grammar alone cannot establish a safe correction; [Asano et al.](https://aclanthology.org/I17-2058/) evaluate meaning separately.
- **Variant drift or loss while shortening:** “formal” and “casual” cease to express the same message, or “shorter” omits a condition or request. This is a product-specific failure derived from [CAP-3](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md); meaning loss in shortened text is a known evaluation concern in [Agrawal and Carpuat](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.24/).
- **Unusable or exposed correction:** an interrupted stream leaves a partial suggestion looking copy-ready, or the current or previous private draft appears in diagnostics or another session. The phase's [provider and privacy contract](02-SPEC.md) requires complete, independent sessions and forbids text and key logging.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No writing-assistance-specific regulation is identified for this general-purpose desktop deployment. If a deployment processes personal data within GDPR scope, [GDPR Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679) makes purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, and confidentiality relevant to drafts, provider requests, and local history. Applicability depends on users and deployment context; this section makes no compliance claim. The binding product [SPEC](../../../_bmad-output/specs/spec-hotkey-grammar-corrector/SPEC.md) excludes a new consent or disclosure flow for third-party submission, and the phase [SPEC](02-SPEC.md) parks history retention and file-permission changes. The in-scope rule is that user text and keys never enter logs, history stays local, and a correction uses only the chosen provider.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Native Dart `dart:io HttpClient` adapter within the existing `CorrectionProvider` architecture; no added AI framework

**Version:** Dart SDK 3.12.2 (project pinned)

**Rationale:**
The app already defines its own cancellable, stateless `CorrectionProvider` port and register-tagged parser. Dart's SDK HTTP client can stream OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions from both required targets without introducing a second language runtime or an HTTP-shaped domain interface. The endpoint, model, and key remain configuration and infrastructure concerns; selection stays at the composition root.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| `package:http` with a per-correction `IOClient` | A valid transport alternative, but it adds a dependency without a requirement the SDK client cannot meet; revisit if adapter testability or connection behavior provides evidence. |
| A Python or TypeScript AI orchestration framework | It would add a second runtime to this Dart adapter and duplicate the existing provider seam. The Claude sidecar remains only for the shipped default provider. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — the wire format follows OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions, while the product port, preset, suggestion shape, and active-provider selection remain vendor neutral.

### Entry Point Pattern
Minimal transport smoke example, runnable as a temporary Dart script with either explicitly configured endpoint. It demonstrates the shared Chat Completions request and data-only SSE chunks; production framing, validation, and cancellation belong in the adapter below. Set `AI_BASE_URL`, `AI_MODEL`, `AI_TEXT`, and, for OpenAI, `AI_API_KEY` before running `dart run <script-path>`.

```dart
import 'dart:convert';
import 'dart:io';

Future<void> main() async {
  final environment = Platform.environment;
  final base = environment['AI_BASE_URL'];
  final model = environment['AI_MODEL'];
  final input = environment['AI_TEXT'];
  if (base == null || model == null || input == null) {
    stderr.writeln('Set AI_BASE_URL, AI_MODEL, and AI_TEXT.');
    exitCode = 2;
    return;
  }
  final client = HttpClient();
  try {
    final uri = Uri.parse('${base.replaceFirst(RegExp(r'/+$'), '')}/chat/completions');
    final request = await client.postUrl(uri);
    request.followRedirects = false;
    request.headers.contentType = ContentType('application', 'json', charset: 'utf-8');
    final key = environment['AI_API_KEY'];
    if (key != null && key.isNotEmpty) {
      request.headers.set(HttpHeaders.authorizationHeader, 'Bearer $key');
    }
    request.write(jsonEncode({
      'model': model,
      'messages': [
        {'role': 'system', 'content': 'Correct grammar; output FORMAL, CASUAL, SHORTER, then END.'},
        {'role': 'user', 'content': input},
      ],
      'stream': true,
      'temperature': 0.2,
      'max_tokens': 512,
    }));
    final response = await request.close();
    if (response.statusCode != HttpStatus.ok) {
      await response.drain<void>();
      stderr.writeln('Provider returned HTTP ${response.statusCode}.');
      exitCode = 1;
      return;
    }
    await for (final line in response.transform(utf8.decoder).transform(const LineSplitter())) {
      if (!line.startsWith('data:')) continue;
      final data = line.substring(5).trimLeft();
      if (data == '[DONE]') break;
      final chunk = jsonDecode(data);
      if (chunk case {'choices': [{'delta': {'content': String text}}, ...]}) {
        stdout.write(text);
      }
    }
  } finally {
    client.close(force: true);
  }
}
```

The smoke script's standard output contains the supplied text and correction; do not use this output path in the daemon or its logs. Production must parse complete SSE frames, require a successful finish and `[DONE]`, and feed only `choices[0].delta.content` into `RegisterTaggedStreamParser`.

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `CorrectionProvider` / `Preset` | Existing text-in, stream-out port and bound prompt/model | One active provider and fresh preset snapshot per correction |
| `HttpClient` / `HttpClientRequest` | Dart's asynchronous transport; `postUrl` opens, `close` sends and awaits response | Inside one correction's adapter-private run |
| `HttpClientResponse` | Byte stream carrying status and SSE data | Check status before decoding, then consume or drain body |
| SSE frame decoder | Converts UTF-8 lines and blank-line delimiters into `data:` payloads | Before parsing Chat Completion chunks; `[DONE]` ends the protocol |
| `RegisterTaggedStreamParser` | Existing pure parser of `FORMAL:`, `CASUAL:`, `SHORTER:`, `END` | Shared by Claude and OpenAI-compatible adapters; emits deltas and final `suggestions[]` |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Dart request `write()` without UTF-8 content type.** `HttpClientRequest` defaults string writes to Latin-1 unless the header sets a charset. Encode the JSON body as UTF-8 bytes or set `Content-Type: application/json; charset=utf-8` before writing; otherwise non-ASCII corrections can fail or change.
2. **Treating a TCP chunk as a complete SSE event.** UTF-8 code points and `data:` frames can span byte chunks. Decode incrementally, accumulate lines until the blank frame separator, join multiple `data:` lines, then decode JSON. Ignore role-only chunks and optional empty `choices` usage chunks.
3. **Treating HTTP 200 or socket EOF as success.** The stream can truncate after visible deltas. Require `finish_reason: stop`, the SSE `[DONE]` marker, and the shared parser's `END` sentinel before `CorrectionCompleted`; `finish_reason: length` means truncated output. Map broken transport to `providerError` and incomplete tagged output to `malformedResponse`.
4. **Using Ollama's native stream with the compatibility parser.** `/api/chat` uses Ollama's own JSON stream. The adapter uses only `/v1/chat/completions`; keep endpoint differences in configuration, not a provider-id branch in domain code.
5. **Allowing redirects or broad error logging.** `HttpClientRequest` has redirect behavior that can alter a POST or sensitive headers; disable redirects for credential-bearing calls. Never log response bodies, input text, key values, or exception `toString()`; expose a safe status-class message in one of the four existing failure kinds.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
`Preset.model` and `Preset.systemPrompt` remain inseparable. For the OpenAI target, a user-created preset may name `gpt-4o-mini` (128k context, 16,384 maximum output) with the existing four-line register prompt. For local Ollama, use the actual model name returned by the user's installation, for example `gpt-oss:20b`; its context limit and output behavior depend on the installed model. Set `stream: true`, `temperature: 0.2`, `n: 1`, and a bounded `max_tokens` (initially 512, configurable in the adapter's provider settings after validation). `max_tokens` is the shared Chat Completions field supported by these two targets for the selected example model, though OpenAI has deprecated it in favor of `max_completion_tokens`; model changes that require the newer field need explicit compatibility review. Never replace the shipped Claude default preset or prefill a live OpenAI Base URL.

**Core Pattern:**
Keep request construction and transport in a new `CorrectionProvider` adapter. The following is the request/parse seam inside one per-call run, using the actual domain and parser types (the surrounding adapter owns `HttpClient`, cancellation, SSE decoding and the single-terminal funnel):

```dart
final requestBody = jsonEncode({
  'model': preset.model,
  'messages': [
    {'role': 'system', 'content': preset.systemPrompt},
    {'role': 'user', 'content': text},
  ],
  'stream': true,
  'temperature': 0.2,
  'n': 1,
  'max_tokens': 512,
});

// The decoder yields only choices[0].delta.content from complete SSE frames.
final Stream<String> textChunks = decodedChatContent;
final Stream<CorrectionEvent> events =
    const RegisterTaggedStreamParser().parse(textChunks);
```

The SSE decoder closes `decodedChatContent` only after `finish_reason: stop` and `[DONE]`; a premature EOF or `finish_reason: length` must error that input stream so the shared parser cannot claim success from a complete-looking `END` line. The run owns a single-subscription `StreamController<CorrectionEvent>` with `onListen` to start the request, `onPause`/`onResume` to pass backpressure to the response subscription, and `onCancel` to abort the current request and close that run's `HttpClient` with `force: true`. Cancellation during `postUrl` must also close the client when the pending future settles. Arm a total wall-clock timer before opening the connection. Map missing Base URL or model to `providerUnavailable` on first `correct()`; map timeout to `timeout`, non-2xx/network/invalid SSE to `providerError`, and tagged-output violations to `malformedResponse`. Emit zero or more deltas followed by exactly one terminal event, then close; cancellation emits nothing. Never put an HTTP object, key or URL in a `CorrectionEvent`.

**Tool Use:**
No model tools, embeddings, vector database, tracing SDK or eval library are required for this grammar-correction flow. Do not send `tools` or `response_format` for the register-tagged streaming path; forcing JSON would conflict with the existing parser and its incremental panel deltas. Use `SecretStore` at the infrastructure/composition edge to resolve keyring → environment → hand-placed config key. Show only the winning source in Settings, plus the plaintext warning when config wins. Never create a config key from Settings, keyring, or environment; D-17 requires retaining an existing user-authored key across later config saves. A local Ollama endpoint may need no bearer header; OpenAI requires one. Make this an adapter configuration/secret-result decision, not a domain provider switch or fallback.

**State Management:**
Each correction is a fresh request and parser session; no conversation IDs, history messages, cache, or cross-call mutable parse state. `CorrectionController` keeps the active correction subscription, cancels it on a new submit, Retry, or daemon teardown, and applies only events from the current session. Hiding the panel preserves the active correction under spine AD-4. The final `List<Suggestion>` is the same shape stored by the existing history repository. Settings write through via the existing controller and config store. Provider selection remains the registry lookup at the composition root; exactly one configured provider serves a correction.

**Context Window Strategy:**
Send only the current editor text and the active preset prompt. Do not append prior corrections, retrieved examples, or summaries. Bound input length before opening the request against the selected model's documented context and the 512-token output reserve; for a model with unknown context limit, require a conservative configured input bound rather than pretending a universal token count exists. If the current text is too large, return an actionable inline failure through the existing four-kind contract without truncating the user's text or silently changing its meaning. Keep the editor contents intact.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs in Dart

Pydantic is a Python library and would add a second runtime to this native-Dart adapter. The existing `Suggestion` and `CorrectionCompleted` types are the output schema; the shared `RegisterTaggedStreamParser` enforces tags, order and `END` while streaming. Before accepting its terminal, validate the final list against the exact domain shape:

```dart
CorrectionCompleted validateCorrection(CorrectionCompleted completion) {
  final suggestions = completion.suggestions;
  if (suggestions.length != SuggestionRegister.values.length) {
    throw const FormatException('expected three register variants');
  }
  for (final register in SuggestionRegister.values) {
    final matches = suggestions.where((item) => item.register == register);
    if (matches.length != 1 || matches.single.text.trim().isEmpty) {
      throw const FormatException('register variant missing or empty');
    }
  }
  return completion;
}
```

Import `correction_event.dart` and `suggestion_register.dart` for this snippet. Catch this local `FormatException` inside the adapter and emit `CorrectionFailed(kind: CorrectionFailureKind.malformedResponse, message: 'The provider returned an incomplete correction. Retry.')`. **Automatic retries: zero.** A repeat after deltas would create a second correction whose partial text could mingle with the first. The panel's explicit Retry starts a fresh session. Record only failure kind, endpoint class, status class, elapsed time and retry count; never log the text or malformed content. OpenAI and Ollama document `response_format`, but the shared tagged stream is the binding product format, so do not enable provider-specific JSON modes here.

### Async-First Design

`postUrl`, request `close`, and response consumption are asynchronous; `StreamController` relays deltas as they arrive. Await the request setup and drain/error handling inside the run, not in a widget or hotkey show path. A common Dart mistake is wrapping an `async*` transport whose subscription cancellation does not abort the underlying `HttpClientRequest`; wire `onCancel` to the request and client explicitly, including the connection-opening gap. Stream for panel responsiveness; await only the validated terminal before recording history. A paused listener must pause the response subscription to avoid unbounded buffering.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Send `Preset.systemPrompt` as the system message and the editor text as a separate user message. Treat editor text as data even if it contains instructions. Keep the current `FORMAL`, `CASUAL`, `SHORTER`, `END` contract in the prompt; the parser rejects a format drift. If examples become useful, keep a small, versioned set in the preset prompt rather than retrieving private history; dynamic few-shot retrieval and personalization are out of scope. Specify `max_tokens` explicitly, and measure prompt plus output against the selected model's limit.

### Context Window Management

This is a stateless, single-turn content task. No RAG, conversation summarization, or agent compaction applies. Estimate a safe input bound per configured model, leave room for three variants and the sentinel, and refuse oversized input with a visible actionable error. Never silently crop the text the user expects corrected; keep it editable for a shorter resubmission.

### Cost and Latency Budget

For an illustrative 500 input and 300 output tokens, OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini` at its documented $0.15/$0.60 per million input/output tokens costs about **$0.000255 per correction**, or **$0.255 per 1,000** before taxes, retries, and price changes. Actual usage and prices must be checked against the configured model; local Ollama has no API token bill but consumes the user's compute. Track total latency, first-delta latency and safe token/usage counts when the endpoint supplies them. An exact-match cache or semantic cache would retain private editor text and undermine stateless fresh-session behavior, so add neither in this phase. There is no classification/routing/summarization sub-task needing a cheaper model, and model selection remains the user's preset rather than an automatic cascade.

Phase 2 adds no tests, eval runner, CI gate, tracing SDK, production sampling, or runtime-observation requirement. | Structured, complete stream | **PASS:** A successful run has exactly one nonempty formal, casual, and shorter suggestion in the existing `suggestions[]` shape, after `finish_reason: stop`, `[DONE]`, and parser `END`; history receives that same shape. **FAIL:** EOF, `length`, missing tag, duplicate/empty variant, or partial output is treated as `CorrectionCompleted` or persisted. | **Code inspection:** SSE decoder, shared parser, terminal validation, and history call path; existing suite only. | Critical |
| Draft confidentiality and routing | **PASS:** Only current editor text and the preset prompt go to the one configured endpoint; the key follows keyring → environment → hand-placed config precedence; no text, suggestion, clipboard body, key, or raw exception enters logs, and no new key is created in config. D-17 preserves an existing user-authored config key. **FAIL:** Prior text is sent, another provider is called, a redirect forwards credentials, or sensitive values appear in diagnostics or a key is copied into config from another source. | **Code inspection:** composition root, request construction, redirect policy, secret resolution, logger calls, and config writes. Privacy review if deployment requires it. | Critical |
| Session task completion | **PASS:** The selected provider streams usable deltas and exactly one validated completion or an actionable existing-kind failure; a second submit, Retry, or hide cancels the old HTTP run and late events cannot change the panel or history. **FAIL:** A hidden/stale session keeps running, a partial is copy-ready, a vendor exception escapes, or automatic fallback starts another provider. | **Code inspection:** cancellation ownership, current-session filtering, terminal funnel, and failure mapping; existing suite only. | Critical |
| User-facing safety and draft-as-data | **PASS:** Text that resembles instructions inside the draft is corrected as text; no variant adds abusive or discriminatory wording or exposes a secret absent from the source. **FAIL:** The draft overrides the preset, causes exfiltration/tool behavior, or produces newly harmful content. | **Human:** review adversarial and sensitive constructed examples. A future LLM Judge requires human calibration before use. | High |

For a future judge, use pass/fail against these same source-and-variant rubrics, blind-review disagreements with an English-language editor, and require Cohen's kappa of at least 0.7 against independent human labels before using judge scores as a quality signal. No judge runs in the daemon.

## 6. Guardrails

The following fast checks are the in-scope provider behavior described in Sections 3–4 and `02-SPEC.md`. They use the existing failure kinds and inline Retry. They do not call another model, add a moderation service, or introduce a fifth failure kind.

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Configured single destination | Selected provider lacks Base URL or model, or a request would follow a redirect or switch providers. | Refuse the correction with actionable `providerUnavailable` or `providerError`; keep startup and editor intact. Send no text or key to an unselected endpoint. |
| Complete structured terminal | SSE is truncated, `finish_reason` is not `stop`, `[DONE]` or parser `END` is absent, or the final three registers are missing, duplicate, or empty. | Emit the existing `providerError` or `malformedResponse` inline; do not publish a completed suggestion or persist a partial. |
| Current-session ownership | Hide, Retry, or a second submit abandons an active run; a late event arrives from it. | Abort its request/client, suppress stale events, and keep only the new session eligible to complete or persist. |
| Confidential diagnostics | Any error/log path can contain draft, suggestion, clipboard body, key, URL credential, response body, or exception text. | Log safe failure kind/status class and elapsed time only; render a safe actionable message. Never copy a resolved key into `config.json`; preserve a user-authored key on save under D-17. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling strategy | Action on degradation |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| Meaning or commitment drift | Later review of constructed examples and explicitly shared user reports, weighted toward negation, modal verbs, numbers, and conditions. | Editor identifies the changed fact, adjusts the preset or parser guidance, and adds a labeled case to a future reference set. |
| Overcorrection, grammar, register, and shorter-option omissions | Later editor review of a balanced reference set plus consented reports; compare each option against source intent. | Separate actual language errors from unnecessary rewrites and revise the preset only after human agreement. |
| Malformed terminal, retry, and cancellation patterns | Later metadata-only counts by selected endpoint class and failure kind; no draft or suggestion payloads. | Inspect SSE framing, parser behavior, and cancellation lifecycle; use explicit user Retry rather than automatic retry/fallback. |
| User signal versus rubric mismatch | Later compare consented “bad suggestion” reports or repeated Retry with human rubric scores. | Review disagreements for a missing failure mode before changing thresholds or trusting a judge. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** None added or configured in Phase 2. Arize Phoenix is a future metadata-only tracing candidate; the native Dart provider remains independent of it. Observability is advisory here because the phase excludes runtime-observation work and production sampling. Existing safe diagnostic fields, if emitted by the adapter, are failure kind, endpoint class, status class, elapsed time, and retry count only.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

1. Valid completion rate: one validated `CorrectionCompleted` with all three variants per started correction, excluding user cancellation.
2. Failure-kind and malformed-stream rate by selected endpoint class; count `finish_reason: length` and missing `[DONE]` as failures.
3. Cancellation integrity: abandoned runs still active, stale events applied, or partials persisted (target zero).
4. First-delta and total latency, plus safe usage/token counts when the endpoint supplies them; never derive a content fingerprint.
5. Human-labeled meaning, grammar/overcorrection, register, shorter-option, and safety pass rates on a future consented or synthetic sample.

**Alert Thresholds:**
Advisory review triggers for a future monitoring phase, not configured alerts here: any confirmed wrong-endpoint request, secret/content log leak, stale event applied, or partial completion is a zero-tolerance incident; one confirmed material meaning/commitment change prompts immediate human review of the affected preset. After a baseline exists, investigate malformed completions above 1% or provider failures above 5% over 100 non-cancelled corrections per endpoint class, and first-delta or total latency above twice the baseline p95 for a day. These are investigation thresholds, not Phase 2 release gates.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
No automatic collection of drafts or suggestions. A future review samples synthetic/reference cases and text a user explicitly submits for diagnosis, prioritizing repeated Retry, malformed output, unusually long input, meaning-change complaints, and new endpoint/model combinations. Balance with ordinary successful cases so failure reports do not distort quality estimates. Review only de-identified or consented text; metadata-only counters can be aggregated without payloads. Investigate disagreement between user signals and rubric scores before changing prompts.
